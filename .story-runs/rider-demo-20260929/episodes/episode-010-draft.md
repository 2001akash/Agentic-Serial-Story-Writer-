# Episode 10: The Secret of Building 17

The archive map from Maya’s clipping ended at a blank square beneath the lobby. Alex measured the wall behind the lowest shelf: twelve bricks across, then a seam where the mortar had been patched. He pressed the inverted triangle stamped on the 1927 maintenance copy against it.

Nothing happened.

He was about to put the paper away when a click sounded inside the wall. One brick shifted inward. Behind it, a narrow iron latch turned under his thumb, and a section of shelving eased forward just enough to reveal a stairwell descending into darkness.

Alex called Maya. “I found the space on the map.”

“Don’t go in alone,” she said. “Photograph everything first. If Reyes’s copy proves someone altered official files, this could still be a crime scene.”

The steps ended at a low room of blackened brick. The 1923 fire report described the building’s north corridor as sealed after the blaze, but here its original door remained, charred around the handle. Alex photographed the hinges, the soot, and the date carved into the lintel: September 12, 1923.

Behind the door stood no hidden furnace, only a cabinet bolted to the wall. Its metal drawers were labeled by year. The 1925, 1926, and 1927 drawers held copies of the same cold-case reports Maya had found. The last drawer had no year. Inside lay a folded plan of Building 17, its paper brittle at the creases.

The public floor plans showed twelve apartments. This one showed seventeen narrow doorways along a corridor that no longer existed. Each doorway had a name beside it. Alex recognized the residents from his route—the same names on the 2023 register.

He called Maya back and read them one by one. At the bottom, in a different hand, someone had added a final line: “Witness—Maya Patel.”

“That plan is supposed to be from before the fire?” she asked.

“The date is on the corner.”

“Then don’t touch anything else. I’m bringing Reyes.”

The phone light flickered. For one breath, Alex saw a second set of names showing through the back of the plan, pressed into the paper from a sheet that was no longer there. One was Luis Reyes. Beside it, the words “copy removed” had been written in pencil.

Alex backed away from the cabinet. The plan had recorded Maya before she knew about the building. It had recorded Reyes before he took the evidence home. Maybe it was a forgery, updated by someone watching them. Maybe the dates meant something stranger.

From behind the charred door came three measured knocks.

Maya’s voice crackled through the phone. “Alex, I’m at the lobby. Reyes is with me.”

The knocks came again—this time from the other side of the sealed wall.
