# Episode 7: Cold Case

Maya spread the photocopies across the diner table, keeping one hand on the oldest page so the ceiling fan wouldn’t lift it. Alex had sent her a photograph from the archive: a ledger open to September 12, 1925. She had spent the afternoon comparing it with the newspaper microfilm and the city’s cold-case index.

“I found the files,” she said. “Not one case. A sequence.”

The first report described two residents found dead after the 1923 fire, their room locked from the inside. The next file, dated September 12, 1926, listed three more names from Building 17. A 1927 report added four. The causes were marked undetermined, then crossed out and reclassified as accidents. No suspect. No trial. Each file closed with the same phrase: no evidence of a common cause.

Maya tapped the margins. “Different investigators. Same correction in the same ink. Someone changed the classification after the reports were filed.”

Alex studied the names. Several appeared in the old ledger Evelyn had shown him. “Could the deaths be connected to the fire?”

“Maybe. But the dates don’t line up with the fire itself. They happen years later, always on September 12.” She slid over a grainy image of the 1925 article. The inverted triangle sat beside the final paragraph. “I still don’t know what the symbol means. I do know someone kept altering these records.”

She had expected Alex to tell her about another whisper or another impossible door. Instead, he told her what the Whisper had offered: help bringing the dead to peace, in exchange for Alex’s cooperation. Maya’s expression tightened.

“That’s not proof it can help,” she said. “And anyone who asks for your trust before explaining the price is asking you to take a risk.”

She gathered the pages and turned to the last file in the folder. It wasn’t from the 1920s. A fresh tab read BUILDING 17, SEPTEMBER 12, 2023. The public record listed the same date for every resident on Alex’s route. The attached investigator’s note was short: no common cause established; case closed.

“They closed seventeen deaths without naming a responsible party,” Maya said. “That’s not an answer.”

Alex looked again at the list. The names matched the entries he had delivered to. At the bottom, someone had added a line in pencil: MAYA PATEL—WITNESS, INTERVIEW PENDING.

Maya went still. “I’ve never been interviewed about Building 17.”

The folder’s cover bore a date-stamp from the city archive: received tomorrow.
