# Episode 8: Caretaker

Maya laid the cold-case photocopies across the lobby desk. The dates marched down the page: September 12, 1925; September 12, 1926; September 12, 1927. Beside each report, a clerk had typed “accidental death.” Beside each correction, someone had drawn a small inverted triangle.

“This isn’t a pattern until we know the files are genuine,” she said. “And it still doesn’t tell us who changed them.”

The caretaker, Hargrove, had been listening from the stairwell. He stepped into the lobby but kept his hands in his coat pockets. “You’re looking in the wrong records.”

Maya turned. “Then show me the right ones.”

Hargrove glanced at Alex. “You’ve already met the thing in this building. You know the dead aren’t just names on a page.”

“That isn’t evidence,” Maya said.

“No. It’s why I stayed.” He studied the photocopies without touching them. “For years the night staff told stories about a group that met here every September twelfth. They called themselves the Keepers. They believed the fire left something unfinished, and that remembering the dead kept it from spreading.”

Maya’s expression didn’t change. “You’re repeating a rumor.”

“I’m telling you what I heard. I never saw a meeting.” Hargrove reached into his coat and set a folded carbon copy on the desk. Its edges had yellowed; the heading read “Maintenance—September 1927.” The last lines listed a room inspection and three sets of initials. Under them sat the inverted triangle.

Maya took a photograph before opening the sheet. “Where did you get this?”

“Inside the old boiler log, when I started here. I put it back. I shouldn’t have kept a copy.”

The note beside the initials read: “Names entered before doors opened. Witness to be informed when the rider returns.”

Alex felt his throat tighten. That phrase had appeared on the 1925 clipping too. Maya compared the two photographs, then zoomed in on the carbon copy. The initials were blurred, but the paper bore a stamped archive number.

“This could still be fabricated,” she said. “I’ll check the number against the city catalog.”

Hargrove nodded. “That’s fair.” He started back toward the stairs, then stopped. “If the Keepers were real, they weren’t protecting the building. They were waiting for someone to finish the delivery.”

Maya looked up sharply. “What delivery?”

The caretaker’s face tightened. “I don’t know.”

She turned the copy over. In fresh pencil, beneath the archive stamp, someone had written her name: MAYA PATEL. The handwriting matched the note she’d found on the 1925 clipping.
