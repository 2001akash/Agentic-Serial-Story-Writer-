# Episode 3: The Archivist

The triangle on the seventh-floor mailbox looked freshly cut in the morning light. Alex touched its sharp edge. A whisper moved through the empty hall, then stopped at the stairwell.

He followed it down to the fourth floor, to the apartment where he had left Evelyn Lark’s parcel. The city record on his phone gave her death date as September 12, 2023, the same date listed for every resident on his route. The door stood open now. Beyond it, a woman in a dark cardigan waited beside shelves of books. Alex knew her from the name on the parcel, though the room behind her was colder than the hallway.

“You brought my name back to my door,” she said. “Now let me show you what the building kept.”

“You’re Evelyn Lark.”

“I was its archivist long before I curated anything.” Her gaze moved to his phone. “The dates you found belong to two different records. Don’t let the building make them look like one.”

She led him through the apartment to a narrow service door hidden behind a wardrobe. The stairs beyond descended to a locked room beneath the lobby. Evelyn touched the brass plate, and the latch clicked. Inside, dust veiled shelves of ledgers and boxes. Unlike the public records Alex had searched, these books had no official seals. Their covers carried the inverted triangle.

Evelyn opened a ledger to a page marked 1925. Names filled the left column; September 12 appeared beside each one. The next book, dated 1926, repeated the date. So did the entries for 1927. Several lines had been cut from the pages, leaving thin white scars in the paper.

“The fire was in 1923,” Alex said. “Why does the pattern start two years later?”

“That’s what the missing pages were meant to answer.” Evelyn turned to a later section. A fresh sheet had been sewn into the old binding. It listed the residents whose names appeared on Alex’s route, each with the same 2023 death date. The final entry was Maya Patel.

Alex’s phone buzzed in his hand. Maya’s name lit the screen.

“Are you there?” she asked. Her voice was alive, hurried. “I found something in the clipping. The triangle appears beside a list of names. One of them is yours.”

Alex looked down. Beneath the 2023 entries, in newer ink, someone had written his name. Beside it was a blank date—and the words: DELIVERY NOT YET ACCEPTED.

The ledger snapped shut by itself. From somewhere behind the shelves came the scrape of a second book being pulled free.
