# Episode 6: Ashes of the Past

Alex’s route on Avenue B had always felt ordinary—17 stops, a dozen coffee shops, a few broken streetlights. Today, the elevator doors of Building 17 hissed open to a dim stairwell that smelled of old paper and dust. He tucked his phone into his jacket and stepped onto the landing. The brass plaque reading “Building 17” glinted in the weak shaft of light.

He pulled the first package from the back of his bike, a small box with a faded logo. The door was boarded up; a rusted keyhole stared up at him. He tapped the lock with the tip of his bike, listening for a click that never came. On the sixth floor, the mailbox was still marked with the cream‑colored envelope he’d seen earlier that morning—a fresh inverted‑triangle symbol etched into the metal, as if someone had just carved it. Alex’s breath fogged in the cold air.

A whisper—soft, almost a sigh—seemed to curl around him, drifting toward the stairwell. He followed it, heart thudding, until the echo faded against the concrete wall. He was on the fourth floor now, the glass of the lobby reflecting his own face. A figure in a long coat stood behind the glass, eyes flickering in the dim light. Alex caught a glimpse of a woman who looked familiar; she was Evelyn Lark, the archivist who had once curated the building’s records. She turned, and for a moment he thought she was a projection.

“Alex,” she said, her voice low and steady. “You’re getting closer. The triangle isn’t a symbol of death—it’s a promise. The people who died on September 12, 2023, were bound to a secret that began with that fire in 1923. The ledger I showed you… it’s not just names. It’s a covenant.”

He swallowed. Evelyn slid a thin, leather‑bound book toward him. Inside, the pages were filled with entries dated every September 12 from 1925 to 1927, each name accompanied by an inverted‑triangle. The last page, dated 2023, had his own name written in a cramped hand, the triangle freshly inked beside it.

“Why me?” Alex whispered.

Evelyn’s eyes softened. “Because you’re the one who can bring them peace. The Whisper—if you trust me—has been waiting. He promised to help you.”

Alex felt the weight of the book, of the promise. He remembered the message from Maya, the journalist who had warned him of the triangle’s danger. Maya’s voice had been skeptical, but she had also shown him the microfilm copy of the 1925 column, the hidden note that read, *The names were entered before the doors were opened.*

The Whisper’s voice floated up the stairs, a murmur that seemed to come from the floorboards themselves. “Deliver the package,” it said, “and let the promise be fulfilled.”

Alex’s hand tightened on the book. The elevator’s doors opened onto the fifth floor. He stepped inside, the metal doors closing behind him with a soft click. The elevator’s lights flickered, then steadied, casting a pale glow over the cabin. He could hear the faint thud of something below, a heartbeat in the walls.

He pressed the button for the ground floor, the one that would bring him to the basement he had read about in the fire report. The elevator shuddered, then began its descent.

At the bottom, the air grew colder, the smell of wet stone and old timber. He saw a set of stairs leading down into darkness, the corridor lined with peeling wallpaper that bore the same inverted‑triangle pattern he’d seen on the ledger. He could feel the weight of the past pressing against him, a silent chorus of voices waiting for release.

Alex’s phone vibrated—Maya’s name flashing on the screen. She had sent a photo of a new clipping, dated 1925, with a fresh note that read, *What lies beneath the floorboards?*

He looked up at the dim light above, the triangle etched into the ceiling. The Whisper’s voice whispered again, “Answer the question, Alex.”

The elevator doors slid open, and he stepped into the darkness, the promise of the past calling him forward.

*What lies beneath the floorboards?*
