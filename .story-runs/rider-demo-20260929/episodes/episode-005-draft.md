# Episode 5: Unfinished Business

Alex’s bike rattled through the late‑afternoon traffic, the route still a maze of alleys and narrow stairways. The fourth‑floor window of Building 17 stared back at him, its glass a dull mirror of the sky. He leaned in, breath fogging the pane, and saw a faint outline—a woman in a 1920s dress, her profile framed by a shadow that matched the inverted‑triangle mark from the 1925 clipping. The figure flickered, then vanished as if the building itself had swallowed her. Alex’s heart thumped in his chest, a pulse he’d never felt riding a delivery route before.

He flicked his phone to Maya’s message: *Did you see her?* The text blinked in the dim kitchen light. Maya’s face, framed by a stack of microfilm, was tense. She had been skeptical, but her fingers trembled as she traced the triangle on the newspaper page.

The next drop was at the ninth‑floor suite. The door, still bearing the brass plaque of Building 17, was sealed with wax. Alex slid the key in, turned it, and the lock gave way with a sigh. Inside, a single sheet lay on a desk: a fresh page from the 1925 ledger, page 12. The entries were dated September 12 for 1925, 1926, and 1927, each marked with the inverted‑triangle symbol. A new line, 2023, bore Alex’s name, flagged *DELIVERY NOT YET ACCEPTED*.

He lifted the page. The ink was fresh; the triangle’s edges were cut cleanly, as if a hand had carved it. A whisper brushed his ear—low, almost a sigh. He turned, but the hallway was empty, save for the echo of his own footsteps.

In the lobby, Tomas the concierge offered a cup of coffee. His eyes flicked to Alex’s phone, where Maya’s new message pulsed: *The triangle isn’t just a symbol. It’s a promise. The dead will be free if you bring them peace.*

Alex stared at the coffee, then at the glass of the lobby. The ledger in his pocket felt heavier than ever, the names inside weightless and unresolved. He could deliver the packages and leave the dead in their sorrow, or follow the whisper and try to set them right.

The elevator doors hissed open, revealing a corridor lined with portraits of former residents. Their eyes seemed to follow him. A shadow flickered at the far end—someone or something waiting.

He pressed the elevator button. The doors slid shut, and the hum of the machinery seemed to echo the whisper that had guided him.

*Can he trust a ghost?* The question hung in the air as the elevator descended, the ledger clutched tight in his hand, the promise of peace echoing in his mind.
