# Episode 13: Archive of Shadows

Maya waited outside the fourth-floor office with a folder of permit copies tucked under her arm. “The public plans skip from the lobby to the east stairwell,” she said. “No west lobby. But this older permit uses a room number that doesn’t appear on any later map.”

Alex knocked. The door opened onto a narrow office lined with books and archive boxes. No one stood inside. On the desk, a gray ledger lay open beneath a paperweight. Its cover bore the inverted triangle.

Maya stopped at the threshold. “Was someone just here?”

Alex didn’t answer. In the dark window behind the desk, he saw Evelyn Lark’s reflection. She wore the same cardigan from the archive; her face was pale, the fourth-floor corridor visible through her shoulders. When Alex turned, the room was empty.

“You see her?” he asked.

“I see an office and an open book,” Maya said. “Tell me what you see.”

The ledger listed names by year. Entries for 1925, 1926, and 1927 all carried September 12. The reports beside them had been marked accidental deaths, then crossed out and reclassified. A separate section, written in newer ink, listed the Building 17 residents who died on September 12, 2023.

Maya compared the ledger with her permit copies. “These names match the public death records. That’s verifiable. The earlier entries match Reyes’s cold-case summaries, too.”

At the bottom of the 2023 page, Alex found his name beside “DELIVERY NOT YET ACCEPTED.” Below it was Maya’s: “WITNESS—INTERVIEW PENDING.” The letters were pressed so deeply into the paper that the next page held their impression.

“I never wrote this,” Maya said.

“I know.”

She checked the ledger’s spine and found a pocket stitched into the binding. Inside lay a thin slip of tracing paper. The lines matched the floor plan she had brought, except for one narrow room beside the west stairwell. Its label had been rubbed away. Only the number remained: 17.

“A room, not a floor,” Maya said. “That could be the missing west lobby—or a storage space. I’ll compare it with the original permits before we decide.”

In the window, Evelyn lifted one hand and pointed toward the blank margin beside the 1927 entries. Alex leaned closer. A new line had appeared in the dust on the glass: “Ask who opened the door.”

Maya turned at the sound of his breath. The glass was clear to her. Alex looked back at the ledger. Beside the 1927 entry, someone had penciled a time: 11:17 p.m.

Maya photographed the page. “I can verify the room number,” she said. “I can’t verify what you just saw.”

Alex kept his eyes on the penciled time. “Then we find out who opened the door.”
