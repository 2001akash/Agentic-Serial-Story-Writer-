# Episode 9: The Skeptic

Detective Luis Reyes let Maya finish laying out the photocopies before he touched any of them. The cold-case office smelled of burnt coffee and warm printer toner. Alex stood beside the table, trying not to look at the badge clipped to Reyes’s belt.

“You want me to connect three accidental deaths from the twenties to seventeen deaths in 2023,” Reyes said. “And the connection is a triangle?”

“The same date, the same building, the same mark,” Maya replied. “The old reports were changed after filing. We’re asking who changed them.”

Reyes turned to Alex. “And you think a ghost told you this?”

“I think the register had my name on it before I knew it existed.”

“That’s not evidence of a ghost.” Reyes tapped the photocopy of the 1927 maintenance log. “But this is evidence somebody handled the records. Where did you get it?”

“The caretaker found it inside an old boiler log. He says a group called the Keepers met in the building.”

Reyes gave Maya a look. “You believe him?”

“I believe this copy matches the archive stamp. I don’t know what the group believed.” She slid over the city’s 2023 case summaries. “These were all closed as unrelated. I checked the dates against the public death records myself.”

Reyes read the summary sheet, then compared its case numbers with the old maintenance record. One number had been typed over. Under the correction, a triangle showed through the paper. His skepticism didn’t disappear, but his attention sharpened.

“I can’t take an original out of evidence,” he said. “I can take a logged copy.” He made a photocopy, wrote the archive reference on an evidence-transfer slip, and signed it. “I’m checking whether the correction matches our case index. That’s all. No ghosts, no secret society.”

Maya nodded. “That’s all we’re asking.”

Reyes put the copy in a clear sleeve and slipped it into his coat pocket. He paused at the door. “If the records are real, someone wanted them read. That doesn’t mean the message is meant for you.”

“Or for you,” Maya said.

He gave no answer.

At home, Reyes laid the sleeve under his desk lamp. The typed-over case number looked ordinary from the front. He turned the sheet. Pressure marks from a page above had left faint writing in the paper, invisible until the light hit at an angle.

Three words emerged beneath his signature: “REYES—WITNESS NEXT.”

His phone was already in his hand. He sent Alex a photo of the copy, then typed: “I don’t believe your ghost story. But someone knew I’d take this home.”
