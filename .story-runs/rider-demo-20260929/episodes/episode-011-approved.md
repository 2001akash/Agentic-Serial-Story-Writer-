# Episode 11: Echoes of the Past

The returned parcel waited on the lobby desk when Alex came back from his route. Dispatch had marked it undeliverable and sent it back to the building, though the label named no apartment—only “Building 17, care of the archive.”

He carried it down to the basement room. The box was light, its paper softened by damp. Inside lay a funeral program folded around a small brass number tag. The printed date was September 12, 1923. No names appeared on the front, only a black border and a line: “Service held in the west lobby after the fire.”

The moment Alex read it, the archive vanished.

He stood in the lobby as it must have looked a century earlier. Smoke stained the ceiling. Wet coats crowded the walls. A closed coffin rested beneath the brass plaque, while mourners filed past carrying little numbered tags in their palms. No one spoke. At the back, a woman pressed a triangle into the damp plaster with her thumb.

Alex tried to move, but the scene held him. A boy beside the coffin looked up. Soot darkened one cheek. He held out a tag marked 17.

“They wrote us down before they opened the doors,” the boy said.

The present slammed back around Alex. The archive shelves stood where they had been. His hands were empty, but the brass tag lay on the floor beside the program. Its number had been scratched through and replaced with a name: Samuel Ortiz.

Alex photographed both pieces and called Maya. She answered from the records office, papers shuffling near the receiver.

“The date matches the fire,” he said. “The program says the funeral was held in the west lobby.”

“There was no west lobby on the public plans,” Maya replied. Her voice had lost its usual edge of certainty. “Send me the tag. I’ll check the original building permits. Don’t decide what it means yet.”

Alex looked at the parcel label again. The sender field was blank. Beneath it, a faded dispatch stamp read RETURNED: RECIPIENT NOT FOUND.

The elevator groaned overhead. From somewhere above came the measured sound of a funeral bell—three notes, then silence. Alex placed the tag beside the 1927 maintenance copy. The stamped number matched a mark on the plan’s hidden corridor.

He turned the program over. In pencil, someone had added one name beneath the date: Luis Reyes.

The bell rang once more, this time from inside the sealed wall.
