# Episode 4: Paper Trail

Maya chose a diner across Avenue B, where she could see Building 17 through the rain-streaked window. She arrived with a folder under one arm and no recorder in sight.

“You said the archive listed me as dead,” she said. “That’s a serious claim, Alex. A photograph of a page proves nothing.”

He turned his phone toward her. The image showed the 2023 register: seventeen names, each beside September 12. Maya Patel was among them. Beneath the list, his own name had been added in different ink, followed by “DELIVERY NOT YET ACCEPTED.”

Maya studied the picture without touching the phone. “Could be a prop. Could be a forged page. And you’ve been staring at a building full of ghost stories for three days.”

“Then why bring the folder?”

She opened it. Inside lay a photocopy of a 1925 newspaper column, its paper brittle at the fold. The article described an old rumor: every September 12, residents of Building 17 heard a bell from a floor where no bell was installed. The reporter had dismissed it as a children’s tale. In the margin, someone had drawn an inverted triangle.

“I found it while checking the fire coverage,” Maya said. “The clipping from your package is real. This one is in the library’s microfilm archive. I don’t believe in ghosts. I do believe someone has been planting the same mark in records for a hundred years.”

Alex told her about Evelyn’s appearance and the ledgers beneath the building. Maya’s skepticism hardened at the word “ghost,” but she didn’t leave. She compared the triangle on the article to the image on his screen, then turned the page over. A line of type had been hidden in the fold: “The names were entered before the doors were opened.”

“That could mean anything,” she said, though she photographed it before sliding the article back into its sleeve. “Tomorrow I’ll check the original issue and the building permits. If they match, I’ll come back.”

At the door she stopped. “One more thing. The 1925 column mentions a group portrait taken after the fire. The library catalog says the photograph was donated under the name Evelyn Lark.”

Maya looked toward Building 17. Behind its fourth-floor glass, a figure stood motionless in the dark. Alex recognized the outline of Evelyn’s cardigan. When he blinked, the window was empty.

Maya hadn’t seen it. She was already turning the clipping over again, studying the triangle.

On its reverse, beneath the reporter’s initials, a later hand had written two words: “For Maya.”
